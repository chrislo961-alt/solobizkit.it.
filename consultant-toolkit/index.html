A practical workflow from numbers to finished work

Use the tools as a sequence instead of isolated calculators.

Set your minimum viable rateStart with annual income, business costs and realistic billable hours. Your minimum sustainable rate should come from your own economics before you compare market benchmarks.
Turn the rate into a project priceEstimate the work, include direct costs and add a contingency for scope risk. Define what is included so revisions do not quietly destroy margin.
Confirm scope before billingUse an estimate or written scope before work begins. When the agreed milestone is billable, create an invoice with an exact due date and clear payment instructions.
Review actual performanceCompare estimated hours with actual hours and review how quickly invoices are paid. Feed that information back into your next quote.

Consultant Toolkit FAQ

What should a consultant charge per hour?

There is no universal hourly rate. Start with the annual income, business costs and billable utilization your business requires, then compare that result with relevant market benchmarks.

Should consultants charge hourly or by project?

Hourly pricing is transparent when scope is uncertain. Project pricing can be easier for clients when deliverables are clear. The better model depends on scope risk, value and how predictable the work is.

What should be included in a consulting invoice?

Include seller and client details, invoice number, issue date, due date, line items, totals, payment terms and payment instructions required for your situation.

When is SoloBizKit Pro useful?

Use the free tools for one-off jobs. Pro becomes useful when you want customer history, estimates, invoices, recurring work, reminders and payment status in one workspace.
