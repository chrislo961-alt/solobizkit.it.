How to use this calculator

Use a normal selling price after routine discounts. Volume is held constant so you can isolate the price effect. Test lower sales volumes separately because customers may respond to a price change.

The formula

New price = current price × (1 + increase %). Added revenue = (new price − current price) × unchanged sales volume.

Worked example

Increasing a $50 price by 8% produces a $54 price. At 1,000 unchanged sales, revenue and gross profit rise by $4,000 before any change in demand or operating cost.

What to include

  • The price customers normally pay
  • A realistic planned percentage increase
  • Sales volume for one consistent period
  • Variable cost per sale for the margin calculation

How to interpret the result

Projected profit lift equals added revenue only because variable cost per sale and sales volume are held constant. It is a scenario, not a demand forecast. Review customer segments, competitor positioning and contract notice requirements before applying a change.

Keep definitions consistent when comparing periods. Results depend entirely on the values and assumptions entered.

Frequently asked questions

Does a price increase always raise profit?

No. Profit can fall if sales volume drops enough or costs rise. Test several volume scenarios before deciding.

Should tax be included in the price?

Use either tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive values consistently for both current price and cost analysis.

Can this be used for hourly services?

Yes. Treat the hourly rate as the price and billable hours as sales volume.
