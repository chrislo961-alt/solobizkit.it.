How to use this calculator

Use new paying customers, not leads, trials or total active accounts. Include spending from the same acquisition period. If the sales cycle is long, compare cohorts so current spend is not divided by customers created by much earlier activity.

The formula

SaaS CAC = (sales spend + marketing spend) ÷ new paying customers. Payback months = CAC ÷ (monthly ARPA × gross margin).

Worked example

With $20,000 in sales and marketing spend and 100 new customers, CAC is $200. At $99 monthly ARPA and 80% gross margin, estimated payback is about 2.53 months.

What to include

  • Paid advertising, content, events and marketing software
  • Sales salaries, commissions and sales tooling allocated to acquisition
  • Only net-new paying accounts in the matching cohort
  • Recurring revenue and gross margin from the same customer segment

How to interpret the result

CAC by itself does not say whether acquisition is healthy. Compare it with gross-profit lifetime value, retention and cash available to fund the payback period. Blended CAC can also hide large differences between paid, organic and outbound channels.

Keep definitions consistent when comparing periods. Results depend entirely on the values and assumptions entered.

Frequently asked questions

Should founder sales time be included?

Include a reasonable cost when you want fully loaded CAC. Excluding it can make early acquisition appear artificially cheap.

Do free trials count as customers?

No. Use new paying customers unless you are intentionally calculating cost per trial instead.

Why use gross margin in payback?

Revenue is not fully available to recover CAC. Gross margin estimates the revenue remaining after the direct cost of delivering the service.
