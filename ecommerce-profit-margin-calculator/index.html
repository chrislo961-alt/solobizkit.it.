How to use this calculator

Use net revenue after refunds and discounts, or include those deductions consistently in another field. Match every cost to the same period. If inventory purchases differ greatly from products sold, use cost of goods sold rather than cash paid for new inventory.

The formula

Ecommerce operating profit = revenue − product cost − advertising − platform/payment fees − shipping/fulfillment − other store costs.

Worked example

A store with $50,000 in revenue and $34,500 in entered costs produces $15,500 in operating profit, equal to a 31% operating margin.

What to include

  • Cost of the products actually sold
  • Paid media and creator acquisition costs
  • Marketplace, platform and payment-processing fees
  • Outbound shipping, packaging, fulfillment and returns handling

How to interpret the result

A profitable store can still have cash-flow pressure because inventory is often purchased before revenue arrives. Use this margin view for operating performance, then review inventory timing and cash flow separately.

Keep definitions consistent when comparing periods. Results depend entirely on the values and assumptions entered.

Frequently asked questions

Should shipping charged to customers count as revenue?

Yes, if it is recorded as revenue in your accounts. Also include the shipping expense so both sides are represented.

Where do refunds go?

Use revenue after refunds or enter refund cost consistently in other costs. Avoid counting the same refund twice.

Is this gross margin or net margin?

This is an operating-style margin based on the costs you enter. For gross margin only, use revenue and product cost in the main Profit Margin Calculator.
