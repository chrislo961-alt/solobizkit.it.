What is an invoice?

An invoice is a document used to record and communicate a bill for products, services or other transactions. It identifies who is issuing the invoice, who is being billed, what the charge relates to and how much is due. Depending on the transaction and jurisdiction, it can also contain tax information, payment terms and other required business details.

What should an invoice include?

The exact requirements vary, but a clear invoice commonly contains the following information:

  • Your business, seller or freelancer name and relevant contact details.
  • The customer or client's billing information.
  • An invoice number or other required identifier.
  • The issue date and, where relevant, a payment due date.
  • A description of each product, service, project or billable item.
  • Quantity, hours, unit price or rate and line-item amounts where appropriate.
  • Subtotal, agreed discounts and applicable tax.
  • The final amount due and currency.
  • Payment terms, instructions and useful notes.

Invoice numbers and numbering systems

Invoice numbers make individual invoices easier to identify, organize and retrieve. A simple system might combine a prefix, year and sequence, for example INV-2026-0001. The important point is to use a format that works for your records while following any invoice-number requirements that apply to your business.

Products, services and line items

Use separate line items when an invoice contains several products, services, milestones or billable periods. Each line should describe the charge clearly enough that the customer can understand it. For hourly work, quantity can represent hours and rate can represent the hourly rate. For products, quantity and unit price can be shown separately.

Invoice due dates and payment terms

A due date tells the customer when payment is expected. Payment terms describe the agreed timing or conditions for payment. Common business arrangements may use immediate payment or a defined number of days, but the terms on the invoice should reflect the agreement with the customer and applicable requirements.

Tax, VAT, sales tax and discounts

Tax treatment varies significantly by jurisdiction, business status and transaction. An invoice may need to show tax rates, tax amounts, registration identifiers or other information depending on the rules that apply. Discounts should also be shown clearly so the customer can understand how the final amount was calculated.

Important: SoloBizKit provides practical document and calculation tools, not accounting, tax or legal advice. Invoice, tax, VAT, sales-tax, numbering and record-retention requirements vary by location and situation.

Invoice FAQ

What is the difference between an invoice and a receipt?

An invoice generally communicates an amount being billed or due, while a receipt generally records that payment has been received. The exact terminology and required documents can vary by transaction and jurisdiction.

Does an invoice need a number?

A number or identifier is useful for organizing invoices, and specific numbering requirements may apply depending on where and how you operate.

Should an invoice include a due date?

A due date is useful when payment is not due immediately because it clearly communicates when payment is expected. Whether specific date or payment-term information is required depends on your situation.
