Getting paid

How to handle late-paying clients

Late payment is easier to manage when you use a calm, consistent process instead of waiting until frustration decides what to send.

Check the invoice first

Before chasing payment, confirm that the invoice was sent to the right person, the amount is correct, the due date has passed and any purchase-order or reference requirement was included.

Use a simple escalation sequence

Friendly reminder.

Shortly after the due date, send a polite note with invoice number, amount, original due date and a copy or link to the invoice.

Clear second follow-up.

If there is no response, ask for a specific payment date and whether anything is blocking approval.

Firm overdue notice.

State that the invoice remains unpaid, reference the agreed terms and explain any next step you are actually prepared and legally entitled to take.

Decide the business response.

Depending on the relationship and local rules, that can include pausing new work, changing future terms, requesting deposits or seeking professional collection/legal advice.

Do not invent late fees, penalties or collection rights. Whether they can be charged depends on the agreement and applicable law.

Keep communication factual

Use dates, invoice numbers, amounts and agreed terms. Avoid emotional or accusatory language. The goal is to get a clear response and payment while preserving useful evidence of what was communicated.

Prevent repeat problems

  • Confirm billing contact and purchase-order requirements before work starts
  • Use clear payment terms in the estimate or agreement
  • Invoice promptly
  • Use deposits or staged payments for larger projects when appropriate
  • Track due dates and reminders instead of relying on memory
  • For repeat late payers, reconsider credit terms before accepting more work
