Getting paid

How to handle late-paying clients

The best payment-chasing system is calm, factual and repeatable. You should know what to send, when to send it and when software is worth using to keep the process under control.

Check the invoice before chasing

Use a simple escalation sequence

Friendly reminder.

Send shortly after the due date. Assume a normal admin delay until you know otherwise.

Clear second follow-up.

Ask for a specific payment date and whether anything is blocking approval.

Firm overdue notice.

State that payment remains outstanding and reference the agreed terms.

Business decision.

Decide whether to pause new work, change future terms or seek professional collection/legal advice.

Copy-ready reminder templates

Subject: Invoice [number] – payment reminder Hi [Name], Just a quick reminder that invoice [number] for [amount] was due on [date]. I have attached/sent the invoice again here for convenience. Could you confirm the expected payment date? Thanks, [Name]
Subject: Invoice [number] remains overdue Hi [Name], Invoice [number] for [amount], due [date], is still showing as unpaid on my side. Please let me know whether anything is blocking payment and confirm the date payment is expected. Thanks, [Name]
Before you pay for invoice-reminder software

Check whether it closes the loop

Automated reminders are only useful if they are connected to the actual customer, invoice and payment status.

Due-date visibilityCan you see which invoices need attention without maintaining another list?
Reminder historyCan you tell what was sent and avoid duplicate or mistimed chasing?
Payment statusCan you stop chasing an invoice once it is marked paid?
Customer contextCan you see the customer, estimate and invoice history together?

Keep a payment record

Record invoice sent date, due date, reminder dates, customer replies, promised payment dates and any dispute raised. This prevents the situation becoming an argument about memory.

Prevent repeat problems

  • Confirm billing contact and PO requirements before work starts
  • Use clear payment terms in the estimate or agreement
  • Invoice promptly
  • Use deposits or staged payments for larger projects when appropriate
  • Track due dates and reminders
  • For repeat late payers, reconsider future credit terms
