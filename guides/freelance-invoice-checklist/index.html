Invoicing

What should be on a freelance invoice?

Learn the workflow first, then decide whether a free generator is enough or whether paid invoicing software will genuinely save time.

Invoice and tax requirements differ by country and business type. Use this guide operationally and verify mandatory local fields before issuing real invoices.

Build the invoice in this order

Identify both parties.

Your legal/business details and the customer's correct billing information.

Identify the document.

Unique invoice number, issue date and due date.

Describe the work.

Use line items the customer can recognize against the quote or agreement.

Calculate totals.

Quantity, rate, subtotal, applicable tax/VAT and total due.

Explain payment.

Currency, terms and clear payment instructions.

Before you pay for invoicing software

Look for workflow, not a prettier PDF

Almost any tool can produce an invoice. The paid value should come from reducing repeated admin around the invoice.

Customer recordsCan you reuse client details instead of typing them into every new document?
Estimate → invoiceCan approved work become an invoice without rebuilding line items?
Status and remindersCan you see what is draft, sent, overdue or paid and follow up consistently?
Recurring billingCan repeat work be generated without recreating the same invoice every month?
Payment flowCan customers get a clear payment path and can you track payment status?
Data and securityCan you understand where business/customer data is stored and how access is controlled?
A free/manual setup may be enough when

You send only occasional invoices, have very few repeat customers and are comfortable tracking sent dates, due dates and payments yourself.

Software starts earning its keep when

You retype customer details, send repeat invoices, forget follow-ups, maintain separate estimate/invoice files or cannot quickly tell what is still unpaid.

Create a repeatable numbering system

Use a format you can continue without confusion. Examples include 2026-001, 2026-002 or a simple sequential number. Whatever you choose, keep it consistent with the rules that apply to your business.

Make the due date explicit

“Net 14” can be useful, but writing the actual due date removes ambiguity. If the customer needs a PO number, cost center or reference, collect it before sending.

Before sending

Open the PDF you are actually about to send — not just the editor. Check recipient, amount, tax, due date, payment details, page breaks and attached files.
