Invoicing

What should be on a freelance invoice?

A clear invoice should make it obvious who is billing whom, what was delivered, how much is due, when it is due and how the client can pay.

Invoice and tax requirements differ by country and business type. Treat this as an operational checklist and verify the mandatory fields where you operate.

Core invoice information

Use line items the client recognizes

“Design work – 1” is less useful than “Landing page design and responsive implementation.” Clear descriptions reduce questions and make the invoice easier to match against an estimate or purchase approval.

Use consistent invoice numbers

Pick a numbering system you can continue without confusion. The exact legal rules vary, but operationally the important thing is that each invoice can be identified and traced later.

Make the due date explicit

“Net 14” can be useful, but an actual due date removes ambiguity. If the client needs a purchase order, reference number or specific billing address, collect it before sending the invoice.

Do not guess tax treatment

Use the tax or VAT rules that apply to your business and customer. A generic invoice generator can calculate the percentage you enter; it cannot decide whether that percentage is legally correct.

Before you send

Check the recipient, amount, tax, due date, payment details and attached files. Then save your own copy. A two-minute review is cheaper than correcting an invoice after the client has already entered it into their system.
