Invoicing

What should be on a freelance invoice?

A useful invoice removes ambiguity: who is billing, what was delivered, what is due, when it is due and how the customer can pay.

Invoice and tax requirements differ by country and business type. Use this guide operationally and verify mandatory local fields before issuing real invoices.

Build the invoice in this order

Identify both parties.

Your legal/business details and the customer's correct billing information.

Identify the document.

Unique invoice number, issue date and due date.

Describe the work.

Use line items the customer can recognize against the quote or agreement.

Calculate totals.

Quantity, rate, subtotal, applicable tax/VAT and total due.

Explain payment.

Currency, terms and clear payment instructions.

Core invoice checklist

Create a repeatable numbering system

Use a format you can continue without confusion. Examples include 2026-001, 2026-002 or a simple sequential number. Whatever you choose, keep it consistent with the rules that apply to your business.

Make the due date explicit

“Net 14” can be useful, but writing the actual due date removes ambiguity. If the customer needs a PO number, cost center or reference, collect it before sending.

Before sending

Open the PDF you are actually about to send — not just the editor. Check recipient, amount, tax, due date, payment details, page breaks and attached files.
