How to set up a simple CRM for a small business

A useful CRM is not a giant database. For a solo business, it is a reliable answer to three questions: who is this customer, what happened last, and what should I do next?

Start with the minimum fields

For most freelancers and small businesses, begin with customer name, company, email, phone, status, notes, last contact and next follow-up. Add more fields only when they support a real decision or workflow.

Use a small number of pipeline stages

A practical starting pipeline is Lead → Contacted → Active opportunity → Client, with Won/Lost or Archived where useful. Too many stages make the system harder to maintain than the work itself.

The most important CRM field is often not “industry” or “company size.” It is the next action and when you promised to do it.

Keep notes short and useful

Record facts that matter to the next interaction: scope, budget signals, objections, promised documents, decision dates and preferences relevant to the work. Avoid collecting personal information simply because the software allows it.

Connect sales to documents

A good solo-business workflow should let a customer move naturally from lead to estimate to invoice. That prevents duplicate typing and gives you one place to understand the commercial history.

Set follow-up rules

Decide what happens after a new lead, after an estimate is sent and after an invoice becomes overdue. Consistent follow-up is more valuable than sophisticated automation that nobody checks.

Spreadsheet or CRM?

A spreadsheet can be enough when you have a small number of leads and only one person managing them. Move to a CRM when you repeatedly miss follow-ups, duplicate customer details, need linked estimates/invoices or want a clear activity history.

Privacy matters

Store only information you have a legitimate business reason to keep. Protect login credentials and customer access links, and avoid using a general CRM for highly sensitive or regulated records unless the service explicitly supports that use.

A CRM habit that actually works

Before ending each customer interaction, record one next action or intentionally mark that no follow-up is needed. That single habit turns a contact list into a working CRM.
