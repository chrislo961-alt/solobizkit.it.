Customer management

How to choose a simple CRM for a small business

Learn the workflow first. Then pay for software only when it removes real admin instead of adding another dashboard to maintain.

What a useful CRM must answer

For a solo business, a CRM should answer three questions quickly: who is this customer, what happened last, and what should I do next?

Before you pay for a CRM

Look for less admin, not more features

A long feature list is not useful if you still have to keep the real customer story in email, spreadsheets and separate invoice files.

Fast daily useCan you update a lead or customer in seconds rather than opening a complicated workflow?
Next-action trackingCan every open opportunity have a clear next action and date?
Customer historyCan you quickly see notes, estimates and invoices tied to the same customer?
Estimate → invoice flowDoes the system prevent duplicate typing when a prospect becomes a client?
Reasonable scopeAre you paying for functions your solo business will actually use rather than enterprise complexity?
Data/security clarityCan you understand how account and customer data are stored and accessed?
A spreadsheet may still be enough when

You have a small number of leads, one person manages them, follow-ups are rarely missed and your documents do not need to be linked to customer history.

A CRM starts earning its keep when

You miss follow-ups, duplicate customer data, lose track of estimates, need customer history or keep jumping between separate CRM, quote and invoice files.

Use a tiny pipeline

Start with Lead → Contacted → Active opportunity → Client → Won/Lost/Archived. Add stages only when they change what you actually do next.

Set follow-up rules before you need them

New lead.

Assign a status and next action immediately.

After first contact.

Set a specific follow-up date instead of “remembering later.”

After sending an estimate.

Record sent date, amount, status and next check-in.

After a win.

Move the customer naturally into delivery and invoice workflow.

After a loss.

Record why if known, then archive cleanly.

Use AI to clean notes, not expose sensitive data

Turn these rough sales notes into a concise CRM update with four headings: - Current need - What happened - Next action - Open questions Do not add facts. Keep it under 120 words. Notes: [paste non-sensitive notes]
Do not paste passwords, identity documents, payment-card data, health data or other highly sensitive customer information into an AI assistant just to tidy CRM notes.
