Estimates & quotes

How to write a professional estimate or quote

A good estimate makes the commercial agreement understandable before work starts: what is included, what it costs, what both sides must do and what happens next.

Build the estimate in this order

Identify the parties and project.

Your business, customer, estimate number, issue date and project title.

Define deliverables.

Write what the customer receives, not vague activity.

Price the work.

Show line items where useful, currency, applicable tax treatment and total.

State assumptions and exclusions.

Make invisible boundaries visible before the customer accepts.

Add timing and payment terms.

Start/delivery window, estimate validity, deposit or milestones.

Create an approval step.

Tell the client exactly how to accept.

Write scope as deliverables

WeakBetter
Website workDesign and build one five-section responsive landing page
Social media helpCreate 8 branded post templates and a 30-day content calendar
Consulting90-minute workshop plus written recommendations and one follow-up call

Use this scope template

Project: [project name] Included deliverables: - [deliverable 1] - [deliverable 2] - [deliverable 3] Included revisions: [number/type] Client provides: [content/access/feedback/etc.] Target timeline: [timeline] Not included: [important exclusions] Price: [amount + currency] Payment terms: [deposit/milestones/due date] Estimate valid until: [date] Approval method: [email/signature/online acceptance]

Separate deliverables, assumptions, exclusions, timeline, price and approval. Do not invent services, legal terms, guarantees or client requirements.

Assumptions prevent scope disputes

If the price assumes the customer supplies copy, images, access or feedback by a certain date, say so. If delays change delivery timing, explain that operationally.

Example assumption

“Price assumes the client supplies final approved text and brand assets before production begins. Additional copywriting is not included.”

Exclusions are useful, not negative

Typical exclusions can include hosting, paid media, printing, travel, stock assets, third-party licenses, extra revisions, ongoing maintenance or work outside the listed deliverables.

After acceptance

Keep the accepted version. If the scope changes, document the change and its price/timeline impact rather than silently adding work. Use the accepted estimate as the starting point for the invoice and customer record.
