Estimates & quotes

How to write a professional estimate or quote

A good estimate makes the commercial agreement easier to understand before work begins. The most important parts are scope, price, assumptions, timing and what is not included.

Start with the customer and project

Include your business details, the customer, issue date, estimate number and a short project title. The reader should be able to identify the document without opening an email thread for context.

Describe the scope in deliverables

Write what the customer receives rather than vague activity. “Design and build one five-section landing page with mobile layout” is clearer than “website work.” If revisions are limited, say how many are included.

Show pricing clearly

Use line items when they help the client understand the work. State currency, tax treatment where applicable and the total. If some costs are estimates rather than fixed amounts, label them accordingly.

State assumptions and exclusions

If the price assumes the client supplies copy, images or access by a certain date, write that down. Also state important exclusions: hosting fees, paid media, printing, travel or third-party licenses, for example.

Scope problems often begin with work that one side assumed was included but the other side never priced.

Add timing and validity

Include an expected start or delivery window if known, plus how long the estimate remains valid. This matters when supplier prices, availability or workload can change.

Payment terms

State any deposit, milestone payments or balance due after completion. For larger projects, staged payments can reduce the amount of unpaid work accumulating before the final invoice.

Create a clear approval step

Tell the client how to accept: written email approval, signature, online acceptance or another method appropriate to your process. Keep a record of what version was accepted.

After acceptance

Use the accepted estimate as the starting point for the invoice and project record. If scope changes, document the change instead of silently adding work.
