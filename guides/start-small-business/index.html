Small business startup

How to Start a Small Business: a practical checklist

You do not need a huge business plan before you can start. You do need a clear offer, a way to be found, a price, a way to invoice, a way to get paid and a simple system for following up.

Practical guideReviewed Sep 5, 2026
Rules for registration, tax, VAT and invoicing depend on where you operate. Use this as an operational checklist, then verify local legal and tax requirements.

The simplest useful order

Define one clear offer.

Describe what you do, who it is for and what result the customer gets. “Website design for local trades” is easier to sell than “digital services.”

Choose a workable business name.

Check the name against business registries, trademarks and available domains before investing in branding.

Get a domain and basic website.

A one-page site with your offer, proof, contact details and a clear next step is enough to begin.

Set a price that covers real time.

Include admin, sales, gaps between projects, software, insurance and time off rather than dividing a salary target by every hour in the year.

Create an invoice process before the first sale.

Decide how you number invoices, what payment terms you use and how customers will pay.

Keep a simple customer list.

Track lead, contacted, quote sent, won/lost, next follow-up and notes. That is enough CRM structure for many new businesses.

Your startup checklist

Do not overbuild at the beginning

Many new owners lose time choosing software, logos and complex systems before they have customers. Start with the smallest system that supports selling and delivering. Upgrade when a real bottleneck appears.

Your website can start with one good page. Your CRM can start with a handful of fields. Your pricing model can start with an hourly or project baseline. The goal is to make the business operable, not impressive inside a dashboard.

What to do first this week

Pick the offer, register the domain, publish a basic website and create a list of 20 people or businesses that could realistically need what you sell. Then start conversations. The first useful business system is one that helps you reach a customer.
