Pricing

How to price freelance services without guessing

Your rate has to cover the hours you cannot bill as well as the hours you can. Pricing becomes much easier when you separate your minimum sustainable rate from the price a specific project is worth.

Start with the business math

Add the personal income you need, annual business expenses and a reasonable buffer. Then divide that revenue target by realistic billable hours — not every hour you plan to work.

If you work 40 hours a week but only 60% is billable, pricing as if all 40 hours are paid will understate the rate you need.

Know your floor, then price the project

Your calculated hourly rate is a planning floor. A project price can be higher when the scope is risky, turnaround is short, expertise is scarce or the work creates substantial value for the client.

Hourly, day rate or fixed project?

Hourly

Useful when scope is uncertain or work is ongoing. Make sure clients understand how time is tracked and when you will alert them about overruns.

Day rate

Simple for workshops, consulting days or work that naturally fills most of a day. Account for preparation and follow-up around the booked day.

Fixed project

Best when scope and deliverables are clear. Estimate internal hours anyway so you can check whether the quote is profitable.

Define scope before price

A fixed price without boundaries can become unlimited work. State deliverables, included revisions, timeline, client responsibilities and what counts as extra work.

Do not discount without changing something

If a client cannot afford the price, reduce scope, remove deliverables, extend the deadline or change payment terms. A random discount teaches the client that the original price was negotiable without a reason.

Review pricing with real data

After each project, compare estimated time with actual time, note unexpected costs and update your assumptions. Pricing improves fastest when you learn from completed work rather than copying someone else's rate online.
