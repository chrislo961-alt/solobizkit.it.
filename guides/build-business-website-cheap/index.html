How to build a business website almost free

You can launch a fast, professional business website without paying for expensive hosting. For many freelancers and small businesses, the main unavoidable cost is simply the domain.

Practical guideReviewed Sep 5, 2026

What you actually need

A simple business website needs four things: a domain, hosting, a small number of useful pages and a way for customers to contact you. Everything else is optional until the business proves it needs more.

A basic static site can often be hosted free on services such as GitHub Pages or Cloudflare Pages. Your domain is usually the recurring cost.

A low-cost setup

Buy a domain.

Choose something short, easy to spell and close to your business name. Avoid clever spelling that customers will mistype.

Create a simple site.

You can use a static HTML template, a lightweight site builder or a generated starter page. Keep the first version small.

Host it free.

Static hosting platforms can serve a small site at no monthly hosting cost. Connect your custom domain after the site works on its temporary URL.

Add HTTPS and redirects.

Make sure visitors land on one secure canonical version of the site, not several versions with and without www or HTTP.

Add analytics and Search Console carefully.

Analytics is optional. Search Console is useful for checking whether Google can discover and index your pages.

The minimum pages

Most small businesses can start with Home, Services, About and Contact. Add Privacy if you use analytics, forms, ads or other data-processing features. If you sell online or run a subscription product, your legal and trust pages need to reflect that real functionality.

Home page

Say who you help, what you do and what the visitor should do next. A strong first sentence is more valuable than a complicated animation.

Services page

Describe the problems you solve, typical scope and how a customer gets started. You do not need to publish every price, but vague language makes it harder for visitors to understand the offer.

Contact page

Make the next step obvious: email, phone, booking link or form. Test it yourself before sending anyone to the site.

What makes a cheap site look professional?

  • One consistent font and spacing system
  • Fast loading and mobile-friendly layout
  • Real contact information
  • Clear service descriptions
  • No broken buttons or placeholder pages
  • Simple colors and strong contrast
  • A domain email when practical

SEO basics for a new business site

Give every important page a unique title, description, H1 and canonical URL. Link pages together naturally. Create a sitemap and allow search engines to crawl the public pages. Then focus on useful content and real mentions or links from relevant places rather than trying to manufacture hundreds of thin pages.

When should you pay for hosting?

Pay when you need something the free setup genuinely cannot provide: server-side applications, managed ecommerce, complex databases, guaranteed support, higher operational requirements or a builder your team can maintain more easily. Do not pay just because paid hosting sounds more professional.

A realistic first version

A one-page website with a custom domain, service description, proof, contact details and working HTTPS can be enough to start getting customers. Build version two after users give you a reason to.

Platform pricing and free-tier limits can change. Check the current terms of any hosting or domain provider before relying on a specific cost.
