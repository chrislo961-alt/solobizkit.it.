A simple invoice template for small businesses

A consistent invoice layout can make billing easier to manage and helps customers quickly understand what they are being charged for. The goal is not to make an invoice complicated; it is to present the important transaction details clearly and consistently.

What should a small business invoice include?

  • Your business or seller name and relevant contact information.
  • The customer's name, company and billing information where applicable.
  • A clear invoice number for your records.
  • The invoice issue date and payment due date where relevant.
  • A description of each product, service or billable item.
  • Quantity, hours, unit price or rate and line-item amounts.
  • Subtotal, discounts and applicable tax.
  • The total amount due and the currency.
  • Payment terms and useful payment instructions.

Example small business invoice structure

A straightforward invoice can begin with your business details and the word Invoice, followed by the invoice number and dates. Next, identify the customer, list the products or services in a table, calculate the subtotal and applicable tax, show the final amount due, then finish with payment terms or notes.

Invoices for services and products

Service businesses can describe projects, consulting work, appointments, labor, retainers or billable hours. Product businesses can list individual products, quantities and unit prices. If an invoice contains several products or services, use separate line items so the customer can understand how the total was calculated.

Invoice numbers for small businesses

A consistent invoice number helps distinguish documents and makes records easier to search. Some businesses use a prefix, year and sequence such as INV-2026-0001. Use our free Invoice Number Generator to build a simple numbering format.

Payment terms and due dates

Make the expected payment timing clear. Depending on the agreement, payment may be due immediately or after a defined period. The invoice due date and payment terms should reflect the arrangement with the customer and any requirements that apply to the transaction.

Small business invoice requirements vary

Required invoice information, numbering rules, tax or VAT treatment and record-retention obligations vary by country, business type and transaction. This template is a general organizational resource rather than accounting, tax or legal advice. Check the rules that apply to your business.

Small business invoice FAQ

Can I use this invoice template for services?

Yes. Service businesses can list the service, project, hours or milestone being billed and the corresponding rate or price.

Can I use it for products?

Yes. List each product as a line item with the relevant quantity and unit price, then calculate the subtotal and applicable tax.

Should every invoice have a unique invoice number?

Invoice-number requirements vary by jurisdiction. A consistent identifier for each invoice is useful for record keeping, and some jurisdictions impose specific numbering requirements.
