How to use this calculator

Copy spend, clicks and conversions from the same Google Ads date range and account scope. Decide which conversion action matters before comparing campaigns; a purchase and a page view should not be treated as equivalent outcomes.

The formula

Average CPC = campaign spend ÷ clicks. Cost per conversion = spend ÷ conversions. Conversion rate = conversions ÷ clicks × 100.

Worked example

A campaign with $2,500 in spend, 1,800 clicks and 90 conversions has a $1.39 average CPC, a $27.78 cost per conversion and a 5% click-to-conversion rate.

What to include

  • Total charged ad spend for the reporting scope
  • Valid clicks from the same campaigns and dates
  • The primary conversion action used for the decision
  • Consistent attribution settings when comparing periods

How to interpret the result

Low CPC is useful only when the clicks are relevant. Review search terms, conversion quality, customer acquisition cost and profit alongside CPC. Platform-reported numbers may change slightly after invalid-click adjustments or attribution updates.

Keep definitions consistent when comparing periods. Results depend entirely on the values and assumptions entered.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my CPC differ by keyword?

Auction competition, quality, intent, device, location and match type can all affect click cost.

Is CPC the same as CPA?

No. CPC is cost per click; CPA divides spend by conversions or acquisitions.

Should all Google Ads conversions be combined?

Only if they represent comparable value. For clearer decisions, calculate each important conversion action separately.
