INVOICE WORKFLOW GUIDE

What to do when an invoice is late

A late invoice is usually best handled as a sequence: verify the invoice, send a clear reminder, document the response and escalate only when necessary.

1. Verify the invoice first

Check the client name, invoice number, amount, due date, purchase-order requirements and payment instructions. A simple administrative error can delay payment.

2. Send a short reminder

Reference the invoice number, amount and original due date. Ask for a specific payment date instead of sending a vague “just checking in” message. Use the invoice reminder templates if you need wording.

3. Keep a record

Save emails, call notes and promised payment dates. Consistent records matter if the invoice needs to be escalated later.

4. Be careful with fees and escalation

Do not invent late fees after the fact. Whether interest, fees, collection steps or legal action are appropriate depends on the agreement and jurisdiction. Seek qualified advice when the amount or dispute warrants it.

5. Improve the next invoice

Use a consistent number, explicit payment term and exact due date. The Invoice Due Date Calculator and Invoice Generator can help.
