How to make an invoice step by step

An invoice is a document that records what you are billing a customer or client for and how much is due. A useful invoice should make the transaction easy to understand and give both sides enough information to identify the document later.

1. Add your business or seller information

Start with the name of the business, freelancer or seller issuing the invoice. Add the contact and address information that is relevant to your business and required in your jurisdiction. If you use a business logo, you can include that for branding.

2. Add the customer's billing information

Identify the person or company receiving the invoice. Depending on the transaction, this may include the customer's legal or business name, billing address, email address and other required identifiers.

3. Create an invoice number

Give each invoice a clear identifier so it can be found in your records. Consistent formats such as INV-2026-0001 are easy to recognize. Use the free Invoice Number Generator if you want help building a repeatable numbering format.

4. Add the issue date and due date

The issue date shows when the invoice was created or issued. The due date communicates when payment is expected. Your due date should match the payment terms agreed with the customer.

5. List the products or services

Add a clear description for each product, service, project, milestone or billable period. Where relevant, include quantity or hours, the unit or hourly rate and the amount for each line.

6. Calculate subtotal, discount and tax

Add the line-item amounts to get the subtotal. Apply any agreed discount and then calculate taxes according to the rules that apply to the transaction. Tax treatment varies significantly by jurisdiction, so verify your obligations rather than assuming a generic rate applies.

7. Show the total amount due

Make the final amount easy to find. If your invoice uses a particular currency, identify it clearly so the customer knows exactly what amount is expected.

8. Add payment terms and instructions

Include relevant payment terms and the information the customer needs to pay you. This can include the agreed payment period and appropriate payment instructions. Avoid placing sensitive credentials or passwords on an invoice.

9. Review the invoice before sending it

Check names, invoice number, dates, descriptions, calculations, currency, tax treatment and payment information. Save a copy for your own records according to the bookkeeping and retention rules that apply to you.

What should an invoice include?

  • Seller or business information
  • Customer or client billing information
  • A clear invoice number
  • Issue date and, where appropriate, due date
  • Descriptions of products or services
  • Quantities, hours, rates and line totals where relevant
  • Subtotal, discounts and applicable tax
  • Total amount due and currency
  • Payment terms and useful payment instructions

How to make an invoice as a freelancer

Freelancers can follow the same basic structure. For hourly work, show the billable hours and hourly rate. For fixed-fee projects, list the project, milestone or service and the agreed amount. Our Freelancer Invoice Generator provides a focused starting point.

Invoice rules depend on where you operate

Required invoice fields, numbering rules, VAT or sales-tax treatment and record-retention obligations vary by country, business type and transaction. This guide explains a general invoice workflow and is not accounting, tax or legal advice. Check the requirements that apply to your business.

How to make an invoice FAQ

Can I make an invoice without accounting software?

Yes. You can create an invoice with a document tool or invoice generator, provided the finished invoice contains the information required for your situation and you maintain the records you need.

What is an invoice number?

An invoice number is an identifier used to distinguish one invoice from another. A consistent numbering system makes invoices easier to organize and locate.

Do I need to put a due date on an invoice?

Whether it is legally required depends on your situation, but a due date is useful when payment is not due immediately because it communicates when payment is expected.
