How to use this calculator

Use costs from the same accounting period as sales. Enter refunds and discounts consistently—either subtract them from sales first or include them in other costs, but do not count them twice.

The formula

Operating profit = monthly sales − food and beverage cost − labor − occupancy − other operating costs. Margin = operating profit ÷ sales × 100.

Worked example

With $80,000 in monthly sales and $66,000 in combined operating costs, the restaurant produces $14,000 in operating profit and a 17.5% operating margin.

What to include

  • Food, beverage and packaging cost used during the period
  • Hourly and salaried labor plus employer costs
  • Rent, utilities and occupancy expenses
  • Delivery commissions, software, repairs, marketing and other operating costs

How to interpret the result

The result is only as complete as the cost categories entered. Debt payments, income tax, owner distributions and one-time capital purchases may need separate treatment. Compare months using the same definitions to see whether menu price, waste, staffing or channel mix is changing margin.

Keep definitions consistent when comparing periods. Results depend entirely on the values and assumptions entered.

Frequently asked questions

Is food cost the same as total restaurant cost?

No. Food cost is only one category. Labor, occupancy, delivery fees and other operating expenses also affect restaurant profit.

Should delivery-app fees be included?

Yes. Put commissions and related fees in other operating costs, or calculate delivery sales as a separate channel.

Can I use weekly numbers?

Yes. Use weekly sales and weekly costs throughout; do not mix weekly and monthly values.
