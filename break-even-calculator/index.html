What does break-even mean?

The break-even point is where estimated sales cover the costs included in the calculation. Below it, those costs are not fully covered; above it, the model begins to show a surplus.

Formula

Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ (selling price per unit − variable cost per unit). Real businesses can have taxes, changing costs, discounts and other factors that this simple planning calculator does not model.

How to use the break-even result

Compare the required units with a realistic sales forecast and available capacity. Then test a cautious scenario with a lower selling price or higher variable cost. A result that works only under the most optimistic assumptions is a warning to revisit price, costs or fixed commitments.

Which costs belong in the calculation?

Fixed costs do not change directly with each unit sold, such as rent or a recurring software plan. Variable costs rise with each unit, such as materials, packaging, transaction fees or direct fulfillment. Classifying mixed costs carefully makes the estimate more useful.

Example

With fixed costs of 5,000, a selling price of 50 and variable cost of 20, contribution is 30 per unit. Dividing 5,000 by 30 gives 166.67, so the business must sell 167 whole units to cover the costs in this simplified model.
