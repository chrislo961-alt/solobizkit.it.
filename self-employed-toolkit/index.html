A practical workflow from numbers to finished work

Use the tools as a sequence instead of isolated calculators.

Know your required revenueSeparate desired personal income from business expenses and non-billable time. A salary-equivalent hourly number is usually too low for self-employment.
Choose how to price the workUse hourly pricing for uncertain scope and project pricing when deliverables are clear enough to estimate.
Invoice with exact termsUse a consistent invoice number, issue date, due date, clear line items and payment instructions.
Watch the cash gapRevenue and cash are not the same. Monitor when money actually arrives compared with when expenses leave the business.

Self-Employed Toolkit FAQ

How do I calculate a self-employed hourly rate?

Add the income and business costs you need to cover, allow for a safety buffer if appropriate, then divide by realistic annual billable hours.

Should I include non-billable time in my rate?

Yes. Sales, admin, bookkeeping, learning and unpaid time off reduce the number of hours you can actually bill.

How long should clients get to pay an invoice?

Payment terms depend on the agreement and local norms. Use a specific due date and make sure the client understands the term before or when the work is agreed.

What is the difference between profit and cash flow?

Profit measures revenue minus expenses under the accounting approach used. Cash flow measures actual money entering and leaving the business over time.
