How to use this calculator

Use either users or sessions as the visitor measure and keep that choice consistent. Remove test orders and obvious bot traffic when possible. The target is a scenario, not a promise; traffic quality, device mix and product price all affect conversion.

The formula

Ecommerce conversion rate = orders ÷ visitors × 100. Revenue uplift at target = (target orders − current orders) × average order value.

Worked example

With 40,000 visitors and 800 orders, conversion rate is 2%. At a 2.5% target and $75 average order value, the model adds 200 orders and $15,000 in revenue.

What to include

  • A consistent visitor or session count from one analytics source
  • Completed orders from the same dates and store scope
  • Average order value after discounts and before or after tax consistently
  • A realistic scenario rate for planning

How to interpret the result

Revenue uplift assumes visitor volume and average order value stay unchanged. It does not subtract product cost, advertising or fulfillment, so use the ecommerce profit calculator to translate additional revenue into profit.

Keep definitions consistent when comparing periods. Results depend entirely on the values and assumptions entered.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use users or sessions?

Either can work, but the resulting rate differs. Choose one denominator and use it consistently across comparisons.

Do repeat purchases count?

Orders count as conversions in this model, so one customer may create multiple orders. Use unique customers instead if you want a customer conversion rate.

Does a higher conversion rate always increase profit?

Not necessarily. Discounts, low-margin products and costly acquisition can raise orders without improving profit.
