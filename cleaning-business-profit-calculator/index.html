How to use this calculator

Enter one typical job rather than mixing several service types. If deep cleans and recurring cleans have different prices or labor time, calculate them separately. Include paid travel time when it is a real labor cost.

The formula

Gross profit per job = price − [(on-site hours + paid drive time) × wage × (1 + payroll burden %) + supplies].

Worked example

At $180 per job, 3 on-site hours, 0.5 paid drive hours, a $22 wage, 12% payroll burden and $8 of supplies, direct cost is $94.24 and gross profit is $85.76 per job.

What to include

  • Cleaner wages for all paid time connected to the job
  • Employer payroll taxes, workers compensation or similar labor burden
  • Consumable supplies used for the cleaning
  • The actual price after recurring-client discounts

How to interpret the result

Gross profit is not owner take-home pay. Insurance, software, marketing, vehicles, administration and owner compensation still come out of this amount. Use profit per job to compare service types and identify whether price, route density or job duration needs attention.

Keep definitions consistent when comparing periods. Results depend entirely on the values and assumptions entered.

Frequently asked questions

What costs belong in cleaning-job profit?

Include direct labor, paid drive time, employer labor burden and supplies. Track overhead separately so the result stays useful for comparing jobs.

Should owner labor be included?

Yes, if the owner performs the cleaning. Use a realistic hourly labor cost instead of treating owner time as free.

How do I compare recurring and one-time cleans?

Run a separate scenario for each because price, job time, supplies and acquisition effort can differ.
